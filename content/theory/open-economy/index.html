Open Economy Options

OG-Core offers a wide range of specifications regarding the type and degree of openness assumed in the economy. In none of our specifications do we fully model foreign economies as is done by Benzell et al. (2017) and others. However, one of the findings of Benzell et al. (2017) is that a full multi-country model is closely approximated by the types of large partial open economy specifications we use in OG-Core. Our specifications range from fully closed, to partially closed, to small open economy, to large open economy. We discuss some of these specifications in Chapter Market Clearing. But the open economy assumptions only refer to how foreign capital can flow into the private capital market Kt≡∑m=1MKm,tK_t\equiv\sum_{m=1}^M K_{m,t}Kt​≡∑m=1M​Km,t​ and into the government bond market DtD_tDt​. The labor market and goods markets are closed.

Partially Open Economy¶

The partially open economy is the default specification of OG-Core in which 0<ζK,ζD<10<\zeta_K,\zeta_D<10<ζK​,ζD​<1. In this case, foreign flows of private capital KtfK^f_tKtf​ and foreign holdings of government bonds DtfD^f_tDtf​ partially supply the respective domestic demands for these two types of capital. The equations for this partially open specification are described in Sections Government bond market clearing and Private capital market clearing of Chapter Market Clearing.

The partially open economy specification can also be referred to as a large open economy because changes in underlying policy or parameter assumptions will influence the equilibrium interest rate on private capital rtr_trt​, while the world interest rate rt∗r^*_trt∗​ remains fixed. The degree to which the domestic rental rate on private capital rtr_trt​ responds to policy parameter changes depends on the degree to which the economy is open. In the most open case, the small open economy specification described in Section Small Open Economy with ζK=1\zeta_K=1ζK​=1, foreign flows of private capital KtfK^f_tKtf​ are the most flexible and the domestic interest rate is exogenously fixed at the world interest rate rt∗r^*_trt∗​. As ζK\zeta_KζK​ goes to 0, foreign private capital flows KtfK^f_tKtf​ become less flexible and the domestic interest rate rtr_trt​ has to adjust more to make domestic private capital demand KtK_tKt​ equal total private capital supply Ktd+KtfK^d_t + K^f_tKtd​+Ktf​.

Note that in our partially open economy specification, the world interest rate rt∗r^*_trt∗​ is necessary for determining equilibrium because the foreign supply of private capital KtfK^f_tKtf​ depends on a concept of excess demand that is based on domestic demand at the world interest rate (127). It is also worth noting that our partially open economy specification is not a multi-country model in which the rest of the world or multiple other countries are explicitly modeled. In our specification, the rest of the world is simply modeled as the relationship between the domestic rental rate on private capital rtr_trt​ and the world interest rate rt∗r^*_trt∗​ as influenced by the supply of foreign private capital ζK\zeta_KζK​ and by foreign purchases of new issues of government bonds ζD\zeta_DζD​.
