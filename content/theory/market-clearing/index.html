Market Clearing

M+3M+3M+3 markets must clear in OG-Core---the labor market, the private capital market, the government bond market, and MMM goods markets. By Walras’ Law, we only need to use M+2M+2M+2 of those market clearing conditions because the remaining one is redundant. In the model, we choose to use the labor, private capital, government bond market, and the first M−1M-1M−1 goods market clearing conditions and to ignore the MMMth goods market clearing condition. But we present all M+3M+3M+3 market clearing conditions here. Further, the redundant MMMth goods market clearing condition---sometimes referred to as the resource constraint---makes for a nice check on the solution method to see if everything worked.

We also characterize here the law of motion for total bequests BQtBQ_tBQt​. Although it is not technically a market clearing condition, one could think of the bequests law of motion as the bequests market clearing condition.

Market Clearing Conditions¶

The sections below detail the labor, government debt, private capital, and MMM goods market clearing conditions of the model in the baseline case of a large partially open economy.

Capital markets generalities¶

Before describing the government bond market and private capital market clearing conditions, respectively, we define some general capital market conditions relative to both markets. Both the government bond market and private capital market are characterized by interest rates that differ exogenously. As described in (104), the interest rate at which the government repays debt or earns on surpluses rgov,tr_{gov,t}rgov,t​ differs from the marginal product of capital rtr_trt​ by an exogenous wedge. And the marginal product of capital is determined in equilibrium rtr_trt​ (except in the closed economy case). But we make a simplifying assumption that households are indifferent regarding the allocation of their savings between holding government debt or investing in private capital.[1] And we assume that this indifference exists in spite of the difference in relative returns between rgov,tr_{gov,t}rgov,t​ and rK,tr_{K,t}rK,t​. We define total domestic household savings in a given period as BtB_tBt​.

And total domestic household savings is constrained to be allocated between domestic private capital ownership KtdK^d_tKtd​ and domestic holdings of government bonds DtdD^d_tDtd​.

Ktd+Dtd=Bt∀t K^d_t + D^d_t = B_t \quad \forall tKtd​+Dtd​=Bt​∀t

Government bond market clearing¶

The government in OG-Core can run deficits or surpluses each period, as shown in equation (98) in Section Government Budget Constraint. Because the government can borrow or save on net each period DtD_tDt​, someone must lend or borrow those assets on the supply side.

We assume that foreigners hold a fixed percentage of new domestic government debt issuance. Let Dt+1−DtD_{t+1} - D_tDt+1​−Dt​ be the total new issuance government debt, and let Dt+1f−DtfD^f_{t+1} - D^f_tDt+1f​−Dtf​ be the amount of those new issuances held by foreigners. The government debt market clearing condition is the following, where total domestic government debt DtD_tDt​ equals the amount of that debt held by domestic households DtdD^d_tDtd​ plus the amount of that debt held by foreign households DtfD^f_tDtf​.

Dt=Dtd+Dtf∀t D_t = D^d_t + D^f_t \quad\forall tDt​=Dtd​+Dtf​∀t

If ζD=0\zeta_D=0ζD​=0, we are assuming a closed economy government bond market in which all new government debt is held by domestic households and in which all government debt in the long-run (steady-state) is held by domestic households Dˉf=0\bar{D}^f=0Dˉf=0.

This approach nicely nests the small open economy specification discussed in Section Small Open Economy of Chapter Open Economy Options in which ζK=1\zeta_K=1ζK​=1, foreigners flexibly supply all the excess demand for private capital, the marginal product of capital is fixed at the exogenous world interest rate r∗r^*r∗, and domestic households face the least amount of crowd out by government debt. Total Bequests Law of Motion¶

Total bequests BQtBQ_tBQt​ are the collection of savings of household from the previous period who died at the end of the period. These savings are augmented by the interest rate because they are returned after being invested in the production process.

Because the form of the period utility function in (37) ensures that bj,s,t>0b_{j,s,t}>0bj,s,t​>0 for all jjj, sss, and ttt, total bequests will always be positive BQj,t>0BQ_{j,t}>0BQj,t​>0 for all jjj and ttt.
