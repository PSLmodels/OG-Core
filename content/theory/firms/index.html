Firms

The production side of the OG-Core model is populated by MMM industries indexed by m=1,2,...Mm=1,2,...Mm=1,2,...M, each of which industry has a unit measure of identical perfectly competitive firms that rent private capital Km,tK_{m,t}Km,t​ and public capital Kg,m,tK_{g,m,t}Kg,m,t​ and hire labor Lm,tL_{m,t}Lm,t​ to produce output Ym,tY_{m,t}Ym,t​. Firms face a flat corporate income tax τm,tcorp\tau^{corp}_{m,t}τm,tcorp​ and can deduct capital expenses for tax purposes at a rate δm,tτ\delta^\tau_{m,t}δm,tτ​. Tax parameters can vary by industry mmm and over time, ttt.

Production Function¶

Firms in each industry produce output Ym,tY_{m,t}Ym,t​ using inputs of private capital Km,tK_{m,t}Km,t​, public capital Kg,m,tK_{g,m,t}Kg,m,t​, and labor Lm,tL_{m,t}Lm,t​ according to a general constant elasticity (CES) of substitution production function,

Ym,t=F(Km,t,Kg,m,t,Lm,t)≡Zm,t[(γm)1εm(Km,t)εm−1εm+(γg,m)1εm(Kg,m,t)εm−1εm+(1−γm−γg,m)1εm(egytLm,t)εm−1εm]εmεm−1∀m,t \begin{split} Y_{m,t} &= F(K_{m,t}, K_{g,m,t}, L_{m,t}) \\ &\equiv Z_{m,t}\biggl[(\gamma_m)^\frac{1}{\varepsilon_m}(K_{m,t})^\frac{\varepsilon_m-1}{\varepsilon_m} + (\gamma_{g,m})^\frac{1}{\varepsilon_m}(K_{g,m,t})^\frac{\varepsilon_m-1}{\varepsilon_m} + \\ &\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad(1-\gamma_m-\gamma_{g,m})^\frac{1}{\varepsilon_m}(e^{g_y t}L_{m,t})^\frac{\varepsilon_m-1}{\varepsilon_m}\biggr]^\frac{\varepsilon_m}{\varepsilon_m-1} \quad\forall m,t \end{split}Ym,t​​=F(Km,t​,Kg,m,t​,Lm,t​)≡Zm,t​[(γm​)εm​1​(Km,t​)εm​εm​−1​+(γg,m​)εm​1​(Kg,m,t​)εm​εm​−1​+(1−γm​−γg,m​)εm​1​(egy​tLm,t​)εm​εm​−1​]εm​−1εm​​∀m,t​
(46)

where Zm,tZ_{m,t}Zm,t​ is an exogenous scale parameter (total factor productivity) that can be time dependent, γm\gamma_mγm​ represents private capital’s share of income, γg,m\gamma_{g,m}γg,m​ is public capital’s share of income, and εm\varepsilon_mεm​ is the constant elasticity of substitution among the two types of capital and labor. We have included constant productivity growth rate gyg_ygy​ as the rate of labor augmenting technological progress.

A nice feature of the CES production function is that the Cobb-Douglas production function is a nested case for εm=1\varepsilon_m=1εm​=1.[1]

Ym,t=Zm,t(Km,t)γm(Kg,m,t)γg,m(egytLm,t)1−γm−γg,m∀m,tforεm=1 Y_{m,t} = Z_{m,t} (K_{m,t})^{\gamma_m} (K_{g,m,t})^{\gamma_{g,m}}(e^{g_y t}L_{m,t})^{1-\gamma_m-\gamma_{g,m}} \quad\forall m,t \quad\text{for}\quad \varepsilon_m=1Ym,t​=Zm,t​(Km,t​)γm​(Kg,m,t​)γg,m​(egy​tLm,t​)1−γm​−γg,m​∀m,tforεm​=1
(47)

Industry MMM in the model is unique in two respects. First, we will define industry MMM goods as the numeraire in OG_Core. Therefore, all quantities are in terms of industry MMM goods and all prices are relative to the price of a unit of industry MMM goods. Second, the model solution is greatly simplified if just one production industry produces capital goods. The assumption in OG-Core is that industry MMM is the only industry producing capital goods (though industry MMM goods can also be used for consumption).

Optimality Conditions¶

The static per-period profit function of the representative firm in each industry mmm is the following.

The government supplies public capital Kg,m,tK_{g,m,t}Kg,m,t​ to the firms at no cost. The per-period interest rate (rental rate) of capital for firms is rtr_trt​. The per-period economic depreciation rate for private capital is δM,t∈[0,1]\delta_{M,t}\in[0,1]δM,t​∈[0,1].[2] The δm,tτ\delta^\tau_{m,t}δm,tτ​ parameter in the second-to-last term of the profit function governs how much of capital depreciation can be deducted from the corporate income tax. Note that the last term above represents the benefits from any investment tax credit τm,tinv\tau^{inv}_{m,t}τm,tinv​. While this should be applied to all investment, firms in OG-Core are making static decisions each period about the amount of capital to rent. Taxes enter the firm’s profit function (48) in two places. The first is the corporate income tax rate τm,tcorp\tau^{corp}_{m,t}τm,tcorp​, which is a flat tax on corporate income that can vary by industry mmm. Corporate income is defined as gross income minus labor costs. This will cause the corporate tax to only have a direct effect on the firms’ capital demand decision.

The tax policy also enters the profit function (48) through depreciation deductions at rate δm,tτ\delta^\tau_{m,t}δm,tτ​, which then lower corporate tax liability. When δm,tτ=0\delta^\tau_{m,t}=0δm,tτ​=0, no depreciation expense is deducted from the firm’s tax liability. When δm,tτ=δM,t\delta^\tau_{m,t}=\delta_{M,t}δm,tτ​=δM,t​, all economic depreciation is deducted from corporate income. The investment tax credit is characterized by the parameter τm,tinv\tau^{inv}_{m,t}τm,tinv​ multiplied by a proxy for investment δM,tKm,t\delta_{M,t}K_{m,t}δM,t​Km,t​ by industry mmm, which represents the percent of industry-specific capital that can be credited against profits.

Firms take as given prices pm,tp_{m,t}pm,t​, wtw_twt​, and rtr_trt​ and the level of public capital supply Kg,m,tK_{g,m,t}Kg,m,t​. Taking the derivative of the profit function (48) with respect to labor Lm,tL_{m,t}Lm,t​ and setting it equal to zero (using the general CES form of the production function (46)) and taking the derivative of the profit function with respect to private capital Km,tK_{m,t}Km,t​ and setting it equal to zero, respectively, characterizes the optimal labor and capital demands.

Positive Profits from Government Infrastructure Investment¶

The CES production function in (46) exhibits constant returns to scale (CRS). A feature of CRS production functions is that gross revenue Ym,tY_{m,t}Ym,t​ is a sum of the gross revenue attributed to each factor of production,

Ym,t=MPKm,tKm,t+MPKg,m,tKg,m,t+MPLm,tLm,t∀m,t Y_{m,t} = MPK_{m,t} K_{m,t} + MPK_{g,m,t} K_{g,m,t} + MPL_{m,t} L_{m,t} \quad\forall m,tYm,t​=MPKm,t​Km,t​+MPKg,m,t​Kg,m,t​+MPLm,t​Lm,t​∀m,t
(51)

where MPKm,tMPK_{m,t}MPKm,t​ is the marginal product of private capital in industry mmm, MPKg,m,tMPK_{g,m,t}MPKg,m,t​ is the marginal product of public capital, and MPLm,tMPL_{m,t}MPLm,t​ is the marginal product of labor.[3] Each of the terms in (51) is growing at the macroeconomic variable rate of egytNt~e^{g_y t}\tilde{N_t}egy​tNt​~​ (see the third column of Table 3). Firm profit maximization for private capital demand from equation (50) implies that the marginal product of private capital is equal to the real cost of capital:

MPKm,t=rt+δM,t−τm,tcorpδm,tτ−τm,tinvδM,tpm,t(1−τm,tcorp)∀m,t MPK_{m,t} = \frac{r_t + \delta_{M,t} - \tau^{corp}_{m,t}\delta^\tau_{m,t} - \tau^{inv}_{m,t}\delta_{M,t}}{p_{m,t}(1 - \tau^{corp}_{m,t})} \quad\forall m,tMPKm,t​=pm,t​(1−τm,tcorp​)rt​+δM,t​−τm,tcorp​δm,tτ​−τm,tinv​δM,t​​∀m,t

Firm profit maximization for labor demand from equation (49) implies that the marginal product of labor is equal to the real wage rate:

MPLm,t=wtpm,t∀m,t MPL_{m,t} = \frac{w_t}{p_{m,t}} \quad\forall m,tMPLm,t​=pm,t​wt​​∀m,t

Even though firms take the stock of public capital Kg,tK_{g,t}Kg,t​ from government infrastructure investment as given, we can still calculate the marginal product of public capital from the production function (46).
